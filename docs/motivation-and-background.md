# Motivation & Background

Cells are highly complex networks in which numerous individual molecules interact to enable life. The CM4AI data generation project seeks to map the spatiotemporal architecture of human cells and apply these maps toward the grand challenge of interpretable genotype-phenotype learning.

The spatial location of a protein within a cell is tightly linked to its function. Proteins must locate in the appropriate spatiotemporal context to carry out their individual role [1]. Mislocalized proteins have been linked to a broad range of diseases. Development of generative image models capable of predicting protein expression and distribution in microscope images has begun. This challenge leverages the CM4AI dataset to stimulate the development of better generative image models that can predict protein expression and subcellular distribution in microscope images.

## Bridge2AI:  Cell Maps 4AI
Cell Maps for Artificial Intelligence ([CM4AI](https://cm4ai.org)) is part of the NIH Bridge2AI consortium ([Bridge2AI](https://bridge2ai.org)). Funded by the NIH Common Fund, Bridge2AI is designed to accelerate the ethical and impactful use of artificial intelligence in biomedical and behavioral research by building rich, AI-ready datasets and establishing best practices across data science, ethics, and collaboration. Ultimately, Bridge2AI aims to open new frontiers in how we understand, treat, and prevent disease.

CM4AI has generated comprehensive data sets for hundreds of proteins and genes, starting with chromatin modifiers and metabolic enzymes. Proteomic mass spectrometry has been utilized to map protein-protein interactions of chromatin modifiers. Immunofluorescence staining followed by confocal imaging has mapped the spatial subcellular organization of key proteins, their interactors and key signaling molecules involved in cancer and iPSC differentiation. Genetic perturbation screens via CRISPR/Cas9 have assessed the transcriptome-wide impact of gene perturbations and unravel mechanisms contributing to the maintenance of cell identity. Together, these data provide a comprehensive dataset to inform AI/ML research on systematic genotype-phenotype mapping that can be leveraged to predict changes in protein expression from baseline images.

##Background and state-of-the-art
Several groups have proposed generative models for microscopy to simulate where proteins localize in a cell. These models differ in what they condition on, generate, and generalize to. We position our ProtiCelli model as state-of-the-art for spatial accuracy: on the held-out CM4AI (Bridge2AI) dataset it reproduces subcellular patterns with higher fidelity than prior methods, faithfully enough to reconstruct cell maps [2]. ProtiCelli is a DiT/EDM diffusion model that generates the protein channel from reference morphology. Rather than reading identity from sequence, it retrieves a learned protein embedding from a look-up table of 12,800 proteins, bounding prediction to seen proteins but generalizing to unseen cell lines.

A complementary family conditions on amino-acid sequence through a protein language model, together with reference images, and so generalizes to unseen proteins. CELL-E 2 [3] pairs a sequence embedding with a nucleus reference image to predict a 2D probability-density map rather than a realistic image. CELL-Diff [4] uses a unified diffusion model to generate protein images directly, in either direction between sequence and image. PUPS [5] frames the task as inpainting, combining a protein language model with a landmark-conditioned network to predict single-cell localization, generalizing to unseen proteins and cell lines. Vermeer [6], a channel-adaptive autoregressive model, currently reports the strongest perceptual quality and biological fidelity across proteins, cell lines, and channel configurations. These models predict unseen proteins, but the sequence-to-localization mapping is under-determined: a benchmark of 3,814 human proteins shows the best sequence and protein-language-model combinations still underperform on fine-grained compartments, multilocalizing proteins (nearly half the proteome), and variants that relocalize despite near-identical sequence [7]. Since compartment classification is a coarsening of image generation, this ceiling upper-bounds sequence-conditioned generation regardless of output sharpness. For well-represented proteins, it yields lower spatial precision than image-conditioned generation.

The two families thus trade off along near-orthogonal axes: image-conditioned generation (ProtiCelli) maximizes pattern fidelity and transfers across cell lines but is bounded to seen proteins, whereas sequence-conditioned generation (CELL-E 2, CELL-Diff, PUPS, Vermeer) unlocks unseen proteins at some cost in precision. All current models train on the Human Protein Atlas and/or OpenCell, so any held-out benchmark must exclude not only our own training data but also those corpora, preventing leakage that inflates sequence-based models.
